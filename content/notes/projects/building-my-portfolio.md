I wanted my portfolio to feel more like something you can explore than another page you simply scroll through.

Instead of separating everything into traditional website sections, I built the site around a small desktop environment. Applications open inside reusable windows, the dock acts as the main launcher, and the terminal contains most of the professional side of the portfolio.

## Why a desktop?

A desktop gives each part of the site its own purpose.

The **Terminal** is where projects, experience, skills, and other technical information can live. **About** is intentionally more personal. **Notes** gives me a place to write longer-form posts without turning the rest of the portfolio into a blog.

It also makes the site feel interactive without requiring every interaction to be animated or complicated.

## Keeping it lightweight

Performance has been part of the design from the beginning.

The site uses optimized images, lightweight window interactions, and avoids expensive visual effects where they are not necessary. The goal is to keep the experience polished while still making it run smoothly on less powerful hardware.

For Notes specifically, the posts are just Markdown files stored with the project. The folder structure organizes them, and the application turns that structure into the folders and notes shown in the interface.

That keeps writing a new post simple: create a Markdown file and put it in the right folder.
